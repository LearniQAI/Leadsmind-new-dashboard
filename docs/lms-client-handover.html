1

Course building

This is the strongest area. A creator can take a course from an empty title to a published, sellable product without leaving the platform.

What you can do today

  • Create a course in two short steps — give it a name and web address (on leadsmind.io or your own connected domain), then pick a visual theme. New courses start as a private draft; you publish when ready.
  • Build the curriculum with modules and lessons. Drag to reorder. Set a module to unlock a set number of days after a student enrols ("drip"), require lessons to be done in order, or gate a lesson behind a quiz pass.
  • Add lesson content from twelve building blocks: video, audio, reading document, rich text, slides, a downloadable file, an embedded widget, a live-session link, custom HTML, a quiz, an assignment, and flashcards. Every one of these both authors correctly and shows up correctly for the student.
  • Design each lesson visually in a drag-and-drop canvas builder, or use one of two ready-made lesson layouts ("Standard" and "Deep-Dive"). This is now the one and only way lessons are built — an older, second lesson-editing system, including two placeholder lesson types ("code exercise" and "SCORM package") that looked real but never ran real code or loaded a real course package, has been retired.
  • Build a sales / landing page from three template designs, with real editable sections: learning outcomes, student reviews, an FAQ, an instructor bio, a curriculum outline and pricing. Each section can be shown or hidden.
  • Set pricing — free, one-time payment, subscription (monthly or yearly), or a hybrid — with checkout that also works for buyers who don't yet have an account (guest checkout). See the note at the top of this document and Section 6 — the checkout flow itself hasn't yet been proven with a real test payment.
  • Organise the catalogue with categories — a simple, one-category-per-course list your students can filter the course catalogue by.
  • Build automation rules — "when X happens, send an email / add a tag / enrol them in another course / issue a certificate." All the real triggers (course completed, lesson completed, quiz passed or failed, module completed, new enrolment, certificate issued, student flagged as struggling) fire correctly, and a rule built on one course only applies to that course.
Still being worked on

One automation action, the plain "send an email" action, doesn't fill in placeholders like the student's first name — it sends the literal text {{student_first_name}} instead. The dedicated certificate-delivery email isn't affected by this; any other rule using a placeholder in its email body is.

Two automation actions in the builder's list, "assign certificate" and "enrol in a bundle," are real, but "grant community access" is only partially built — it tags the record, but there's no community-access gate in the product yet for it to actually restrict.

The student portal

The student side is a complete portal, not just a course player.

What a student can do today

  • Dashboard — see enrolled courses, overall progress, quizzes passed and average quiz score, plus a "Continue learning" panel that drops them back into the right course, and for video lessons, the right spot in the video.
  • Course player — work through lessons with progress tracked per content block. Lessons genuinely lock until earlier ones are done, until a drip date arrives, or until a required quiz is passed. Text-only lessons require the student to actually scroll through and spend time on the reading before they count as complete.
  • My Results page — every quiz attempt with score and pass/fail, assignment status (pending, passed, needs revision) with instructor feedback, earned certificates with verify and download links, per-course progress, and a "My Work" list gathering everything not-yet-submitted, awaiting review, sent back for revision, or recently graded, across every course at once.
  • Course catalogue — browse available courses with a search box, a category filter, a free / paid filter, and sorting by newest, price or title. Enrolment state (enrolled, buy, enrol free) shows on each course.
  • Flashcard review — a dedicated area that gathers flashcard sets from every enrolled course. Cards you mark "still learning" come back within minutes; cards you mark "got it" resurface after a few days.
  • Settings — change their display name (kept in sync everywhere, including on certificates), change their password, and turn course-update emails on or off.
Still being worked on

The flashcard scheduling is a simple two-speed system ("soon" vs. "in a few days"), not a full spaced-repetition algorithm. Changing the account email address from the settings page is intentionally not available yet, because that address ties together everything the student owns.

3

Quizzes

There are two separate quiz systems and both work. Grading is always done on the server from the real answer key — a student can't tamper with their score.

What you can do today

  • Lesson quizzes — attached to a single lesson. Set a pass mark (70% by default) and an attempt limit (3 by default). On passing, the lesson is marked complete. When a student uses up all their attempts, they're offered an AI-generated remedial exercise; passing it unlocks another attempt.
  • Module quizzes — a separate quiz covering a whole module. The student can only take it once every lesson in that module is complete (enforced on the server, not just hidden in the interface). It's reachable from the course outline and the player moves the student to it automatically after the module's last lesson.
  • All eight question styles work for students — multiple choice, true/false, short answer, matching, ordering, fill-in-the-blank, code, and file upload. Short-answer and fill-in-the-blank answers are matched with a bit of built-in tolerance for typos and punctuation, and you can optionally turn on AI-assisted marking for either, question by question.
  • AI question generation — generate multiple-choice questions from a lesson's or a module's content with one click, then edit them.
  • Quiz analytics for creators — see every student's attempts, scores, trends and a per-question breakdown, with CSV export, plus a "Needs grading" queue across every course.
Still being worked on

Code-answer questions are graded by matching text, not by running the code. A correct answer written differently from every listed accepted answer will score zero — the builder and the student both see this stated plainly. A quiz containing a file-upload question isn't instant — it waits for a person to grade it, same as an assignment. AI generation still only produces multiple-choice questions.

4

Certificates

Certificates are real, permanent records — not a fresh PDF invented on every download.

What you can do today

  • Students earn a certificate automatically once they've completed every lesson and passed every quiz in a course. The system re-checks both before issuing.
  • It's emailed to them automatically the moment they earn it, with a real download link — no need for you or the student to do anything.
  • Each certificate has a permanent ID — for example LM-3C48-19BA-A4F7C201 — and freezes the student's name, the course title and the issue date at the moment it's first earned, so a later name change or course rename doesn't alter an issued certificate.
  • Anyone can verify a certificate at a public web page using that ID. It confirms the name, course and date and nothing else — no private data is exposed.
  • Students can also download the PDF any time from the course player (at 100%), their My Results page, or the client portal.
  • You can design the certificate — choose one of three built-in styles (Classic, Modern, Editorial), set an accent colour, add a logo and a signature, or upload your own full certificate design and position the text fields on it. This can be set once as a workspace default and overridden per course.
Still being worked on

The Classic template can clip its footer — confirmed by rendering it — when a student has both a very long name and a very long course title at once; the date and verification-ID line can get cut off at the bottom of the page. Modern and Editorial don't have this issue. Custom-uploaded designs have no automatic spacing check between fields — if two fields like name and course title sit close together, a longer-than-expected value can visually overlap the one below it. Worth a quick check with real long names before relying on a custom design at scale. Automatic delivery reaches every course created from now on, or any earlier course where an admin has switched it on from that course's Automations tab.

5

Course themes

Each course can carry one of three genuine visual identities. This is real, live styling per course — not just a colour label.

The three themes

  • Ember — warm and energetic. Near-white background, vivid orange, rounded shapes, a friendly rounded typeface. Built for coaching and creative courses. This is the default.
  • Signal — sharp and high-contrast. Near-black with white cards, a strong crimson, hard edges, a bold headline typeface and a rotated "seal" motif. Built for certification and flagship courses.
  • Grove — calm and natural. Pale sage background, forest green, a warm serif typeface, soft organic corners and a branching progress line. Built for language, wellness and personal-growth courses.

Each theme controls the full palette (background, text, borders, success and error colours), the heading and body typefaces, the corner rounding, and one signature visual element. A theme is applied on the course's public landing page, throughout the student's course player and lesson outline, and in the creator's preview — consistently, from live course data.

6

Checkout & payments — needs a live test before real customer use

This is the one part of the platform we're asking you to treat differently from everything above.

The checkout flow — including guest checkout for buyers without an account — is built, and the code has been read carefully and looks correct: a buyer pays through a secure, hosted Stripe checkout page, and only a verified confirmation from Stripe ever creates the enrolment. But no real payment has ever actually been run through this system, start to finish, by anyone. The specific check that exists to confirm a buyer can't fake their way to a free enrolment has never been executed — only reasoned through by reading the code.

What this means for you

This isn't "probably fine" — it's the one place in the whole platform where a real test, with real (or safely test-mode) money moving through it, matters more than confidence from reading the code. We'd recommend running one before this checkout path is used with real customers.

The bottom line

Course building, the student portal, quizzes, certificates, course themes and automations are all in good shape and can be used with confidence today. A creator can build, price, theme, automate and publish a full course, and a student gets a complete, honest learning experience with progress, results and a verifiable, automatically-delivered certificate at the end.

The one item that stands apart from everything else in this document is checkout and payments. Nothing about it looks wrong — but nothing about it has been proven right either, because it has never carried a real payment. That's the one piece of real, pre-launch work left before this platform should be trusted with real customer money.
